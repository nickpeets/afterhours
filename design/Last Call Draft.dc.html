TURN 7 · THE DRAFT STORM · 7a
SHOW HER
The line is dark all show, and it moves in order — the four on deck are simply whoever queued first, never who's most popular. A chair opens and it becomes the loudest fifteen seconds in the room: those four revealed at once, hearts hammered live, tallies climbing — and she picks blind off pure momentum. Gold is the crowd's voice, and this is the goldest moment in the show. Two indicators, one window: amber for who's ahead, rose for who's accelerating. A single winner is a result; a leader plus a challenger is a race.
DURING PLAY · LINE IS DARK
No faces, no names, no hearts — for anyone, including the room. The queue is a number. Spectator hearts belong to the chairs.
THE STORM · 15s
Faces revealed to the room only. Rapid-fire multi-tap is the mechanic. She sees shapes and climbing numbers.
SERVER OWNS IT
The 15s window, the tallies, the fallback seat, and heart rate-limiting. Clients render and send intents. Mingle clock suspends; mutes never touch the draft.
♥
Why this replaces scouting. Pre-scouted counts meant the draft was a read of numbers set minutes earlier — the crowd had already voted and was watching a result. Revealing all at once turns it into an event with a beginning: nobody has an edge at 0:15, everyone piles in, and the winner is decided by the room in real time. It also removes the perverse incentive to camp the queue instead of watching the show.
