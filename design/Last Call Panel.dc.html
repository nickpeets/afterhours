KILLING THE STORM
Your instinct about the reveal was right, and the bench is what makes it removable.
The 15-second storm existed to solve a problem the bench doesn't have. When the line was dark, the crowd had no stake in who sat down, so we manufactured one: reveal four strangers, panic-tap for fifteen seconds, done. It was loud, but it was a burst — and bursts are exactly what makes a hangout feel busy and empty at the same time. Ninety seconds of nothing, fifteen seconds of noise, repeat.
A permanently scored bench replaces the burst with a slow build. Someone joins the line, takes a bench seat, and starts earning immediately — so by the time a chair actually opens the crowd has been invested in that person for four minutes. The promotion becomes a payoff instead of an event. No modal, no countdown, no interruption to the show: a chair opens, the bench leader rises into it on the camera-flip, and the next in line drops onto the bench. Five seconds, not fifteen.
It also fixes the thing that made TikTok's crowded rooms boring, which is not the headcount — it's that nothing on screen is ranked. Nine equal tiles are wallpaper. Four big scored cells over three small scored cells is a hierarchy you read in one glance, and every face on it has a number that's moving.
What survives from the storm: she still picks blind — the bench renders to her as silhouettes with live counts, never faces. Hearts are still the crowd's only signal that reaches her. Strike pips still ride on each bench card. The mechanic is intact; only the modal is gone.
◔
One thing to watch. A permanent bench means three people are on camera for a long time without a turn, and that's a worse seat than the old invisible queue — you can be publicly ignored. Two guards: the strike rule already caps how long anyone rots there, and bench cells should show a NEXT UP tag on the leader so waiting reads as progress. If retention on the bench turns out bad, rotate it every round instead of on promotion.
