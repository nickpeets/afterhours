During play the line is dark to everyone — no faces, no names, no hearts, not even for the room. It's a number. Chairs see her and each other; she sees the chairs. Spectator hearts belong to the chairs while the show runs.

Then a chair opens and the queue detonates: candidates are revealed to the whole room at once for 15 seconds, hearts are hammered live, and she picks blind off tallies climbing in real time. That storm is the crowd's power moment — the only time line faces exist, and the only signal that ever crosses to her.
Mutes sit on top of this, never under it. A spotlight or a paid floor hold blurs a chair for everyone at once, and while blurred that chair's heart count hides — you can't back who you can't see. Nothing touches the draft: mutes and floor holds are inert during a storm, and the mingle clock suspends until it resolves.
